# 1-DOF System
![[Dumpened_mass.webp|173]]
This system is described by the law:
$$
\begin{matrix}
\mathcal{L}[m\ddot x + c \dot x + k x]=m \mathcal{L}[\ddot{ x}]+ c \mathcal{L}[\dot{ x}]+k \mathcal{L}[x]
\end{matrix}
$$
Using the properties described in [[0.0.2 - Theorem of the Laplace Transformation and its properties]] we can write:
$$
\begin{matrix}
m [ s^2 X(s) - s x(0) -s^0 \dot{ x} (0)] + C [ s X(s)-s^0 x(0)] + K X(s) = F(s)
\end{matrix}
$$
Collecting on the right side the x-dependant elements:
$$
\begin{matrix}
 m s^2 X(s)+ C s X(s) + K X(S)= msx(0)+m \dot{x}(0)+Cx(0) +F(s)
\end{matrix}
$$
the therms with $x(s)$ and its derivatives define the **starting condition**.
$$
\begin{matrix}
X(s) = \frac{m x_{0}s + m \dot x_{0} + c x_{0}}{m s^2 + c s +k}+\frac{1}{ms^2+Cs+k} \cdot F(s)= \\
X_{homog}(s)+X_{part}(s) \cdot F(s)
\end{matrix}
$$
This last form contain the definitions of:
- transfer function $G(s)= X_{part}(s)$ that relates the input to the output changing its amplitute and its phase
- **Homogeneous Solution**: depends only from $x_0$ and $\dot x_0$, describes the free oscillation tha the systems present if no external input is applied ($F(s) = 0$), describes the ==Free Obscillations==
- **Particular Solution**: defined by null initial conditions ($x_{0}=0$ and $\dot{x}_{0}=0$), describes the ==Forced Oscillations==
If the system is **linear** $X(s)$ can be divided in different therms:
$$
X(s) = \frac{A(s)}{B(s)}+\frac{N(s)}{M(s)}U(s)
\rightarrow
\begin{cases}
\begin{cases}
A(s)=0 \\
N(s)=0
\end{cases}
\rightarrow \text{Zeros} \\
\begin{cases}
B(s)=0 \\
D(s)=0
\end{cases}
\rightarrow \text{Poles}
\end{cases}
$$
this notation allow to divide the equation that gives the poles and the one that give zeros.
For porposes that will be explained in the future, we introduce a different notation:
$$
\begin{matrix}
\omega_{n} = \sqrt{ k \over m } \rightarrow \text{natural angular frequency} \\
\xi = {c \over c_{c}} = \frac{c}{2 \sqrt{ km }}=\frac{c}{2 m \omega_{n}}\rightarrow \text{damping ratio}
\end{matrix}
$$
Now we can rewrite $X(s)$ using the new notation:
$$
X_{\text{homog}}(s)=\frac{x_{0}s+2 \xi \omega_{n} x_{0} + \dot{x_{0}}}{s^2+2 \xi \omega_{n}s+ \omega_{n}^2}
$$
and:
$$
X_{part}(s)=\frac{1\over m}{s^2+2 \xi \omega_{n} s+ \omega^2}
$$
## Homogeneous Solution
In Laplace domain the **stationary motion** is described by:
$$
X_{\text{homog}}(s)=\frac{x_{0}s+2 \xi \omega_{n} x_{0} + \dot{x_{0}}}{s^2+2 \xi \omega_{n}s+ \omega_{n}^2}
$$
The poles of the functions are:
$$
p_{1,2}=-\xi \omega_{n}\pm \sqrt{ \xi^2\omega_{n}^2 + \omega_{n}^2}=-\xi \omega_{n}\pm\omega_{n}\sqrt{ \xi^2-1 }
$$
Different kind of poles can be defined based on the value of $\sqrt{ \xi^2 -1}$.
If:
- $\xi=0 \rightarrow p_{1,2}=\pm \omega_{n}j$ Pure Immaginary, multiplicity $=1$;
	- $|p_{1,2}|= \omega_{n}$
- $0<\xi<1 \rightarrow p_{1,2} = -\xi \omega_{n}\pm j \omega_{n}\sqrt{ 1-\xi^2 }$ the immaginary multiplicator is called $\omega_{d}$ **dampened natural angular frequency**
	- $|p_{1,2}|= \omega_{n}$
- $\xi=1 \rightarrow p_{1,2}=-\omega_{n}$ multiplicity$=2$
- $\xi>1 \rightarrow p_{1,2}=-\xi \omega_{n} \pm \omega_{n} \sqrt{ \xi^2-1 }$ only $\mathrm{Re}$ solution.
![[Oscillating_mass_solution_in_C_space.webp|383]]
We can divide $X_{homog}(s)$ in terms having as denominator a function of the poles:
$$
X_{\text{omog}}(s) = \frac{R_{1h}}{s-p_{1}}+\frac{R_{2h}}{s-p_{2}}
$$
in this way the transformation to time functions is easier.
Assuming that $x_0=0, \dot x_0 = 0$ to simplify the calcultions, the residual are:
$$
\begin{matrix}
R_{1h} = [(s-p_{1})X_{homog}(s)]\Big|_{s=p_{1}}=\frac{\dot{x}_{0}}{p_{1}-p_{2}} \\
R_{2h} = [(s-p_{2})X_{homog}(s)]\Big|_{s=p_{2}}=\frac{\dot{x}_{0}}{p_{2}-p_{1}}
\end{matrix}
$$
This allows us to obtain the form in the domain of the time $X(s) \rightarrow x(t)$:
$$
x_{homog}(t) = \mathcal{L}[X_{homog}(s)]= \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left[\frac{R_{1h}}{s-p_{1}}+\frac{R_{2h}}{s-p_{2}}\right]
$$
Knowing that:
$$
\mathcal{L}[t^ne^{at}]=\frac{n!}{(s-a)^{n-1}}
$$
therefore:
$$
x_{homog}(t)=R_{1h}e^{p_{1} t}+R_{2h}e^{p_{2}t}
$$
The residual contains information about the initial state of the system, while the zeros describe the nature of the system.
### Assumptions

## Particular Solution
$$
X_{s}=\frac{1 \over m}{s^2 + 2 \xi \omega_{n} s + \omega _{n}^2}F(s)
$$
$$
\mathcal{L}[f(t)]=\mathcal{L}[F_{0} 1(t)]= F_{0}{1\over s}
$$
$$
X_{part}(s) = {F\over m} \frac{1}{s^2+2 \xi \omega_{n} s+ \omega_{n}^2}{1\over s}
$$
The poles of the particular solution are:
$$
\begin{cases}
p_{1,2}= - \xi \omega_{n} \pm \omega_{n} \sqrt{ \xi^2 -1 } \\
p_{3} = 0
\end{cases}
$$
$p_3$ is introduced by $1\over s$. Rewriting the particular solution scomposing it to factorial components:
$$
X_{part}(s)=\frac{F}{m}\frac{1}{(s-p_{1})(s-p_{2})s}=\frac{R_{1p}}{s-p_{1}}+\frac{R_{2p}}{s-p_{2}}+\frac{R_{3p}}{s}
$$
Even in this form the first two factors contain the system poles, while the last output therm include a reshape versione of the input.
### The three residual
$$
\begin{matrix}
R_{1p}={F\over m}\frac{1}{(p_{1}-p_{2})p_{1}} \\
R_{2p}={F\over m}\frac{1}{(p_{2}-p_{1})p_{2}} \\
R_{3p}={F\over m}\frac{1}{p_{1}p_{2}}
\end{matrix}
$$
Revriting:
$$
\begin{matrix}
x_{part}(t)=\mathcal{L}\left[X_{part}(s)]=\mathcal{L}^-1[{R_{1p}\over s-p_{1}}+{R_{2p}\over s-p_{2}} + {R_{3p}\over s}\right] \\ \\
=R_{1p}e^{p_{1}t}+R_{2}e^{p_{2}t}+R_{3p}e^{p_{3}t}
\end{matrix}
$$
We know that $p_{1,2}$ and $R_{1,2}$ are conjugates.
### Assumptions
$0 \le \xi \le 1 \ \ \  p_{1,2}=-\xi \omega_{n}+j\omega_{d}$ 
$$
\begin{matrix}
R_{1p}={F_{0}\over  m}\frac{1}{2j \omega_{d}}\frac{1}{-\xi \omega_{n}+j \omega_{d}} \\
R_{1p}={F_{0}\over  m}\frac{1}{-2j \omega_{d}}\frac{1}{-\xi \omega_{n}-j \omega_{d}} \\
R_{3p}={F_{0}\over K}
\end{matrix}
$$
From this I can derive the particolar solution in time domain:
$$
x_{part}(t)=2|R_{1p}|e^{-\xi \omega_{n}t} \cos[\omega_{d} t + Arg(R_{1p})]+R_{3p}1(t)
$$

We can calculate modulus and argument of the residual:
$$
\begin{matrix}
|R_{1p}|={F_{0} \over m} \frac{1}{2 \omega_{d}}\frac{1}{\omega_n} \\
Arg(R_{1p})=\sum Arg(\text{singoli termini di }R_{1p})=-{\pi\over 2}+\theta-\pi=\theta-{\pi \over 2}\pi
\end{matrix}
$$
Where $\theta=\arctan(\xi)$.
We can rewrite the particular solution as:
$$
\begin{matrix}
x_{part}(t)=2({F_{0}\over m}{1 \over 2 \omega_{d}}{1 \over \omega_{n}})e^{-\xi \omega_{n}t}\cos(\omega _{d}t+\theta-{3\over 2}\pi)+{F_{0}\over K }1(t) \\
= {F_{0}\over K} \left[1(t)-\frac{e^{-\xi \omega_{n}t}}{\sqrt{ 1+\xi^2 }}\sin(\omega_{d}t-\theta)\right]
\end{matrix}
$$
Thanks to this formulation we can identify the particular solution as a dumpened oscillation with a steady state at ${F_{0}\over K}$

## General analysis
### Hp:
$x_{0}=0$, $\dot x_0\neq 0$ and $0<\xi<1 \rightarrow$ gives us conjugates $\mathbb{C}$ poles
$$
\begin{matrix}
X(s)=X_{homog}(s)+X_{part}(s)= \\
=\frac{\dot{x_{0}}}{s^2+2 \xi \omega_{n}s+\omega_{n}^2}+\frac{{1\over m}}{s^2+2 \xi \omega_{n}s+\omega_{n}^2}{F_{0}\over s} \\
=\frac{R_{1h}}{s-p_{1}}+\frac{R_{1h}}{s-p_{2}}+\frac{R_{1p}}{s-p_{1}}+\frac{R_{2p}}{s-p_{2}}+\frac{R_{3p}}{s}
\end{matrix}
$$
The first 4 factor define two dampened oscilaltion, one for the homogeneous solution and the second for the particular solution. The last therm is a scaled version of the input.
The three elements define these graph:
- Homogeneous solution general graph:![[Homogeneous_graph.webp|283]]
- Particular solution general graph:![[particular_solution_general_graph.webp|279]]

$$X(s) = \frac{R_{1h}}{s-p_1} + \frac{R_{2h}}{s-p_2} + \frac{R_{1p}}{s-p_1} + \frac{R_{2p}}{s-p_2} + \frac{R_{3p}}{s}$$
Where the therms rappresent:
- **Transient Response** ($\frac{R_{1h}}{s-p_1} + \frac{R_{2h}}{s-p_2}+\frac{R_{1p}}{s-p_{1}}+\frac{R_{2p}}{s-p_2}$):
  * Ruled by the poles (if $\text{Re}(p_{1,2}) < 0$ and $p_{1,2}$ are complex conjugates in $\mathbb{C}$), meaning sooner or later the oscillation expires.
  * The time to expiry of the oscillation is described by $a$ and $b$ (see homogeneous solution) because the decay rate is governed by the real part ($\text{Re}$) of $p_{1,2}$, which is identical for both the homogeneous and particular parts. This time define $\tau$ 

* **Steady-State Response** ($\frac{R_{3p}}{s}$):
  * Represents the persistent response driven by the input/forcing function.
