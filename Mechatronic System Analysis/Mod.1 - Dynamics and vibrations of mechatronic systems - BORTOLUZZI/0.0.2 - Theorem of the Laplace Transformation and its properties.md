# Properties
### Time shift
A signal can be translated in time:
$$
\begin{matrix}
f(t) \rightarrow F(s) \\
f(t-t_{0}) \rightarrow F?
\end{matrix}
$$
![[Time_shifted_signal.jpg|408]]
and its Lagrange transformation became:
$$
\mathcal{L}[f(t-t_{0})]=e^{-t_{0}s}F(s)
$$
### Derivation
$$
\mathcal{L}\left[ \frac{d^i}{dt^i}f(t) \right]=s^i F(s)-\sum_{k=0}^{i-1}s^k \frac{d^{i-k-1}}{dt^{i-k-1}}f(t)\Bigg|_{t=0}
$$
Es:
$$
\begin{matrix}
\mathcal{L}\left[\frac{d^3}{dt^3}f(t) \right]=s^3F(s)-s^2f(0)-s^1\frac{d^1}{dt^1}f(0)-s^0\frac{d^2}{dt^2}f(0)
\end{matrix}
$$
### Integration
$$
\mathcal{L}\left[\int_{0}^t f(\tau)d\tau\right]=\frac{1}{s}F(s)
$$
### Convolution
$$
\mathcal{L}\left[ \int_{0}^{+\infty} f_{1}(\tau) \cdot f_{2}(t - \tau) \, d\tau \right] = F_{1}(s) F_{2}(s)
$$
Pay attention that the time index for the second function goes backward.
### Final value

If $\lim_{t \to +\infty} f(t)$ exists and is finite $\implies \lim_{t \to +\infty} f(t) = \lim_{s \to 0} s F(s)$
We have to know that a limit exists ( functions like $\sin(t) \to \frac{\omega}{s^2 + \omega^2}$)

Because $\lim_{s \to 0} \frac{\omega}{s^2 + \omega^2} = 0$, but is not true for $\sin(t)$ that oscillates.
### Initial value

If $\lim_{s \to +\infty} s F(s)$ exists and is finite:

$$f(0) = \lim_{s \to +\infty} s F(s)$$
